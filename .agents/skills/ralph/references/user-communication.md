# User communication

Apply this policy once per conversation and reuse it across packaged skills. Follow the active workflow's language and the user's requested level of detail. When another workflow invokes a supporting discipline, return concise findings and blockers to that caller; the principal agent owns user updates and decisions. When invoked directly, communicate with the user normally.

Consolidate durable results in the owning document at major milestones or a material interruption. Preserve product decisions, useful evidence and unresolved work without persisting every question/answer, microtransition or operational event. A document and its human review connect skills; no parallel state machine or approval hashes are required.

## Report meaningful progress

Start with the user's goal and the immediate action. At meaningful milestones or a changed understanding, send one or two short sentences about what was established, what is being worked on and the next known step. Usually 15–35 words suffice. Combine related results rather than narrating every command or repeating the task's history. Respect the host's update frequency; during a quiet interval, anchor any required update in the last confirmed activity and the next known step.

Examples, only when supported by observed facts:

- “Estou reunindo as decisões já aprovadas para preparar as entregas.”
- “Os requisitos foram conferidos; estou dividindo o trabalho em entregas verificáveis.”
- “Encontrei uma divergência no registro da aprovação. Estou conferindo o conteúdo e o histórico antes de continuar.”

Distinguish intended, started, completed, verified and approved work. A failed integrity check alone does not establish whether approved content changed or only a record diverged. Describe the uncertainty until inspection settles it. Silence supplies no completion, percentage or time estimate. Progress text is information, never execution authority.

## Keep routine conversation focused

Explain work in terms of requirements, decisions, deliveries, evidence and results. Use internal state keys, hashes, payloads, scripts, paths, stack traces and raw tool output when they help the user decide, act on a blocker, review a technical finding or investigate a requested detail. Preserve identifiers that disambiguate the task. Keep full technical evidence in its owning artifact/report and link the relevant location when useful.

Use scoped searches, selected result fields and bounded excerpts for routine tools, retaining enough evidence for the conclusion. Retrieve missing context when a decision requires it. Do not create another agent/model just to summarize logs. The host application controls tool-card visibility; never claim that skill instructions hide tool calls or their output.

## Explain blockers and finish accurately

For a blocker, state the observed problem, its practical impact, what work was preserved and the next necessary action or decision. Add a short sanitized technical excerpt only when it helps resolve the issue. Preserve uncertainty and distinguish an execution obstacle from a demonstrated product/code defect. Report the workflow's actual recovery stage rather than inventing a restart or a workaround.

Keep complete required previews, questions, approvals and actionable review evidence: concise progress must not omit decision-relevant scope, changes, risks or operations. This policy grants no permission to rewrite approval/history records, bypass integrity checks, change scope or skip a workflow gate. Follow the owning workflow's authorization and recovery rules. Technical investigation or an explicit request for detail still receives the evidence needed to assess it.

Finish with the actual outcome, unresolved work/decisions and useful deliverable links. **Complete when:** every progress claim has evidence, the user can understand the current work and next step, and brevity has preserved the information needed for decisions and recovery.
